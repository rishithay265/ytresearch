What the audience could earn

Actual earnings, sponsor contracts and ad eligibility are unverified for every ranked channel. A public Shorts play count can include starts and replays. Shorts revenue depends on eligible engaged views after a channel joins YPP and accepts the Shorts monetization module, audience geography and the creator pool allocation. YouTube pays creators 45% of their allocation. Pre-acceptance views do not earn Shorts ad share.

As of 20 November 2026, full ads eligibility ordinarily requires 1,000 subscribers plus either 10M valid public Shorts views within 90 days or 4,000 valid public long-form watch hours within 12 months, followed by review. YouTube has announced that from 1 February 2027, new entrants will need 1,000 subs plus 20M qualified Shorts views in 90 days or 8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days. It also announced a 10M rolling qualified-Shorts-view minimum for monthly Creator Pool earnings from that change. A channel can meet the public subscriber target and earn zero on its launch spike.

Three 30-day launch experiments

These are alternatives for one lean team. The cost caps assume shared shoots/assets across Shorts and long videos. Track actual labor and direct costs against each cap. If funding only one, choose Oka Order for reach or Logicz for a business you can monetize through your existing technical expertise.

Oka Order

12 original Shorts and one recipe film. One cook, one voice/actor, one editor. Target 45–75 seconds. Planning cap ₹30,000 including ingredients and labor.

  1. Days 1–3: Secure one kitchen. Pick six dishes across three price bands. Prepare paired scripts with the same dish and payoff, one deadline opening and one guest-objection opening.
  2. Days 4–15: Publish six Shorts in three matched dish pairs, alternating the opening assignment and posting slot. Show the finished plate at the same elapsed second. Record first-frame hold and the dip at the first cooking cut.
  3. Days 16–23: Publish four fresh conflict/dish combinations. Hold duration within ten seconds. Test a visible clock versus a spoken deadline in the stronger opening. Publish one 5–7 minute real recipe for the dish with the most specific viewer questions.
  4. Days 24–30: Run two new-dish replications. Compare 48-hour and eligible seven-day cohorts, subs per 1K engaged views and recipe clicks. Advance only if two different dishes hold the lift without the top Short.

Logicz • Show Me the Bill

10 Shorts and two 6–10 minute screen-recorded tests. One engineer and one editor or the same person. Planning cap ₹20,000 including a ₹3,000 metered-API ceiling.

  1. Days 1–3: Freeze a PDF set and agent task. Log input count, region, tool version, latency, costs, failures and reruns. Prepare a shareable scrubbed log and the same visual receipt card.
  2. Days 4–13: Publish six Shorts in three paired workloads. Alternate opening with the bill versus opening with the failed output. Show the same full result in each. Attach the first long-form benchmark and downloadable test inputs.
  3. Days 14–23: Release two fresh tests and a second full benchmark. Track long-form starts from the linked Short and qualified downloads. Write the first 48-hour results before adjusting the thumbnails.
  4. Days 24–30: Repeat the two winning openings on new inputs for two Shorts. Compare clicks per 1K engaged views and cost per qualified download. Continue only if sign-ups or benchmark interest persist across two workloads.

Ooru Ledger

Eight Shorts, two 6–9 minute owner interviews and two downloadable calculation sheets. Reporter/host, editor and three nearby operators. Planning cap ₹25,000 including travel and consented filming.

  1. Days 1–4: Recruit a tiffin counter, sports turf and florist. Agree which records can be shown. Separate ingredients, waste, rent, wages and owner time. Let operators check figures and label unverified inputs.
  2. Days 5–14: Release four Shorts using two matched business pairs. Alternate opening with selling price versus closing-time loss. Keep calculation order, episode length and thumbnail typography fixed. Publish the first full calculation.
  3. Days 15–23: Release two Shorts on a third business and the second owner interview. Test onscreen arithmetic versus a receipt reveal. Track return viewers, questions that refer to a specific cost, and worksheet downloads.
  4. Days 24–30: Publish two fresh transaction examples using the stronger opening. Compare replies from owners and interviews booked per 1K engaged views, alongside reach. Advance only if economics-based engagement repeats across at least two businesses.
Decision rule for all three pilots. Pre-register episode length, language, posting slots, primary metric and commercial outcome. Compare fixed 48-hour and 7-day windows. Label D24–D30 releases as immature for seven-day comparisons until D31–D37. Use paired medians, remove the best performer once and rerun. A practical continuation gate is a ≥20% relative lift in the chosen outcome in at least two independent pairs, without a fall of more than five percentage points in average percentage viewed. These are management thresholds, not YouTube standards or evidence of causality. If numbers are too thin or results disagree, run two further pairs before scaling spend.

Log views and engaged views separately, percentage who stayed to watch, average view duration and percentage viewed, drop-off at the first scene change, subs gained, traffic source, geography and seven-day follow-on long-form views. For long-form, record impressions and CTR by traffic source. Use a related-video link and a separate tagged landing-page link per episode where available. With near-zero downloads or sales, report counts and denominators instead of unstable percentages.

Scope: global web and public YouTube research through 20 November 2026. Discovery combined new-channel and country rankings, regional-language searches, analytics directories, adjacent content formats, similar-channel listings and direct YouTube recommendations in the Anna cluster. The 35 selected accounts were created within 90 days. This is a purposive case series of winners. There is no denominator of all launches, so it cannot estimate an aspiring channel’s probability of success.

Each ranked entry has a dated creation record, a dated public total and an upload catalog. Thirty-three have retrieved dated history rows; two have only age-bounded totals.
