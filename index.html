02 / Research standards, provenance and missing data

Direct observation: dated title, views, channel snapshots, official policy documents, visible links and dates. Inference: why a title might attract clicks, 1–5 opportunity scores, content gap hypotheses and scenarios. Unknown: true watch-time curves, A/B thumbnails, CTR, traffic-source mix, exact paid sponsorship/affiliate outcomes, private RPM, verified income, deletion/rebrand histories.

Snapshot range: November 10–20, 2026 for deeper channel feeds; broader channel directory November 16, 2026; other source dates noted. Videos selected from August 22–November 20, 2026. Some source URLs are snapshots from aggregators, not direct authenticated YouTube API responses. Monetization claims are not validated by raw view counts.

  • first_upload_date: earliest observed accessible upload for Tube Wala only. Limit: incomplete upload-history search, so not definitive across history.
  • video_views: source public third-party per-video count at source snapshot. Limit: lifetime-to-snapshot; not 28/90-day incremental.
  • views_per_day: public lifetime views divided by elapsed calendar days through snapshot. Limit: approximate lifetime velocity, not measured daily or rolling-7d velocity.
  • views_vs_channel_sample_median: video lifetime views divided by 48 recent-uploads median where available. Limit: mixed age denominator; age-biased and not a true channel-relative anomaly score.
  • top100: 100 views-ranked entries drawn from 117 screened niche-adjacent candidates from 7 main feeds. Limit: NOT the global/platform-wide top100; omits long-tail + many competitors.
  • 20 video audit: 20 individually written public-evidence breakdowns. Limit: 0:00–0:30 direct playback not obtained; title/thumbnail hypothesis not verified spoken script.
  • last20 uploads: audited_recent_uploads.csv recent niche-relevant sample for named feeds. Limit: NOT complete last20 all-upload audit of all 52 channels.
  • income: creator screenshots or audited payouts not available. Limit: public view counts cannot validate $ claimed in titles.
  • Google Trends US: Veedio normalization of Google Trends. Limit: 0–100 relative index; NOT absolute number of searches; derived site has unrelated related-query errors.

Counts do not mean completeness: A 52-channel discovery list does not imply every channel has reliable first-upload dates or 20-video analysis. A 100-video ranking is the top 100 by displayed total video views within the 117 candidate videos collected, not the top 100 across all YouTube. Duration-based format cannot prove official Shorts classification. Media thumbnails are linked via video IDs but their exact visible wording/composition was not separately authenticated in this run.

GLOBAL ENGLISH / MIXED AUDIENCES

AI animation, cost breakdowns, earnings audits

Grow With Kaz’s November 4 Claude Code animation upload had 492,488 views against its 14,095 48-upload median (34.94×, unadjusted for age). Best differentiated formats: experiments with receipts, working files, actual net-profit ledgers, repeatability tests, and audit of affiliate-funnel claims.

Demand vs supply — what the data can and cannot say

Sampled uploads repeatedly cover monetization policy, faceless/AI workflows, “niches” discovery and earnings claims. Repetition establishes topic supply; strong view counts on some uploads establish audience response for those specific videos. Neither alone establishes monthly YouTube keyword volume, universal demand growth, or unsaturated competition. The headline US Trends series is cooling, while new Claude workflows are episodically spiking: the two signals can coexist because they measure different behaviours.

Repeated unmet questions — evidence and uncertainty

  1. “Is this $10,000/month from AdSense alone or affiliate/course income?” Public titles seldom establish source splits. Reddit user-reported experiment meta-discussion explicitly complains that real outcomes differ from headline numbers; small unrepresentative sample.
  2. “How many hours, credits, revisions, license fees and failures went into this AI tutorial?” “Free plugin” titles do not establish that all production was free.
  3. “Which 2027 requirement applies to me, and if I am already monetized, am I removed?” Answer must quote the official YPP page, not creator hearsay.
  4. “Does a faceless AI video fail monetization automatically?” No blanket ban established; originality and policy context matter.
  5. “How much will 100,000 India-heavy views earn?” Channel RPM, geography, format, monetization eligibility and ad-fill unknown; no precise estimate justified without Studio.
  6. “Did a truly new channel do these numbers, or an old channel/rebrand?” Distinguish joined date, first visible upload, and verified full history.

What revenue streams are credible?

  • Ads: eligibility-gated and audience-dependent; delayed until thresholds and approval; public counts are not payouts.
  • Affiliate sales: plausible from AI creator tools; real commission and conversion require partner statements. Disclose referral relationships visibly.
  • Sponsorships: plausible for creator SaaS and automation tools if viewers are high-intent; without a negotiated deal, quote no CPM or spend.

11 / 90-day execution, test criteria and actual production constraints

Cadence hypothesis: 2 independently researched long-form videos and 3 original, derived Shorts weekly for 13 weeks = 26 long videos + 39 Shorts. This is a resourcing assumption, not a viral growth formula. A smaller team should halve the schedule before sacrificing proof quality. Build one channel and one clear language lane first. Do not mix Hindi policy explainers and English AI workflow tests randomly on a single channel.

DAYS 1–30 / BASELINE

Proof-first pilot

Publish 8 long videos + 12 Shorts. India option: 2027 policy myth checker (2), faceless eligibility deep dive (2), exact watch-hour math (1), 3 public-video claim audits. Global option: 2 AI tool replicates, 2 cost/ROI breakdowns, 2 creator earning audits, 2 monetization policy explains. Keep source/date at bottom of every graphic.

Measure inside your own Studio: impressions, CTR, average view duration, first-30s retention, traffic mix, new vs returning viewers. Capture changes at 24h / 7d / 28d. No numeric CTR guarantee.

DAYS 31–60 / EXPERIMENT

Repeat and discriminate

8–9 long videos + 12–13 Shorts. Take the strongest 2 topic families by watch hours per production hour, not only views. Re-run matched comparison with one creative variable changed (e.g., proof-first intro vs promise-first intro), keeping titles/descriptions logged. Disclose tests and track income only when actually received.

Kill criterion (provisional): two successive well-executed attempts with clearly below-median own-channel reach and high cost per qualified viewer, unless official news provides unusual evergreen value.

DAYS 61–90 / POSITION

Build defensible series

9–10 long videos + 14–15 Shorts. Develop one repeatable weekly signature series (e.g., “Creators’ Receipts”). Publish downloadable cost/claim worksheets, one mini public data release and update the source ledger. Approach one sponsor only after proving audience fit; disclose paid relationships.

Decision gate: continue if repeatable original series rises on your own channel baseline and viewers demand follow-ups; pivot if reach is dominated by a one-off policy spike with no repeat audience.
